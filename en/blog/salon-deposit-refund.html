Recovering a Deposit When a Business Sale Falls Through: A Beauty Salon Case in Bahrain

By: Attorney Zainab Ali MadanCivil CasesSource: Akhbar Al Khaleej — 14 March 2026

Buying an existing business — such as a beauty salon with all its furniture and equipment — is a common way to enter the market quickly. But the deal can fail for reasons beyond the buyer's control, most notably when the commercial registration cannot be transferred or obtained. What happens to the deposit then?

Case summary

Attorney Zainab Ali Madan's client agreed with the seller to buy a commercial beauty salon with all its contents, and paid an advance as a deposit. However, completing the agreement ran into the impossibility of obtaining the commercial registration required to operate the business, so the buyer went to court to recover what she had paid.

The civil court ordered the seller to refund the deposit: when an agreement becomes impossible to perform it comes to an end, and neither party may keep payment for a deal that never happened.

What this case means for you

  • Check the commercial registration before paying: ask for proof that the registration can be transferred or issued for the same activity before handing over any money.
  • Put the agreement clearly in writing: state in the contract what happens to the deposit if completion becomes impossible for reasons beyond your control.
  • Keep proof of every payment: bank transfers and signed receipts are your first evidence in court.
  • Get advice before signing: reviewing a contract beforehand costs far less than a court dispute afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get my deposit back if the deal does not go through?

That depends on why the deal failed and on what the agreement says. Where performance became impossible for reasons not attributable to you, courts have ordered deposits refunded in similar cases. Every case has its own circumstances, which we review in the consultation.

What documents do I need to sue for a deposit refund?

The sale contract or written agreement, proof that the deposit was paid, any correspondence with the seller, and proof of why the deal could not be completed.
