Acquitted of Embezzlement: How a Malicious Accusation Against a Salon Worker Collapsed

By: Attorney Zainab Ali MadanCriminal CasesSource: Al Watan

A simple employment dispute over wages can turn into a criminal accusation. In such cases the lawyer's role is to expose the background of the accusation and insist that a conviction can only rest on solid evidence.

Case summary

Attorney Zainab Madan's client worked at a women's salon. A dispute arose with the employer over her delayed wages, and he accused her of embezzling beauty tools and face masks, presenting some of his staff as witnesses.

At the appeal stage, the lawyer argued that the accusation was malicious: it came after he had refused to pay her wages and dismissed her, no invoices were produced for the items allegedly taken, and the video footage the complainant claimed to have was never presented. The case ended with the worker's acquittal.

If your employer accuses you unfairly

  • Don't ignore the accusation: ask for a lawyer from the very first stage of the investigation.
  • Document the original dispute: proof of late wages or dismissal can reveal the motive for the accusation.
  • A conviction needs evidence: missing invoices or alleged recordings weaken the accusation.
  • An appeal is a second chance: the first-instance judgment is not the end of the road.

Frequently asked questions

What is a malicious accusation?

An accusation driven by revenge or pressure rather than real facts — for example, one that follows a financial or employment dispute. Proving this can affect how the court weighs the evidence.

Can a conviction be appealed?

Yes, within the legal deadlines. In this case the acquittal was obtained at the appeal stage.
